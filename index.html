About this page
Why this page exists

The frame: this is mostly an economic war, with calibrated kinetic shocks

Neither Tehran nor Washington wants a full shooting war — both have decided it would cost more than either could win. So the slow game is economic. But "slow" doesn't mean "quiet": Iran's May 4 and May 8 missile barrages on UAE infrastructure, the IRGC's attacks on three US destroyers on May 7, and the US Navy's disabling of two Iranian oil tankers on May 8 showed that both sides will throw calibrated kinetic strikes when the economic squeeze gets uncomfortable. The frame is not "no fighting"; it is "fighting kept below the threshold that triggers full war."

Iran's lever is the Strait of Hormuz. Keep it fully closed — as it has been since May 4 — and oil prices stay elevated, Trump pays a domestic political bill in gas-pump prices ($4.52 today, above the $4.50 political threshold) and midterm polling, and U.S. allies in the Gulf get nervous enough to pressure Washington toward concessions.

The U.S. lever is the sanctions architecture, naval pressure, and now direct tanker interdiction. Iran's rial is at 1.842M and rising; oil exports are collapsing via both storage exhaustion and US tanker-disabling; and Tehran pays the bill in street pressure and currency collapse.

The structural deadlock is unchanged: the US demands nuclear moratorium upfront; Iran demands peace and sanctions relief first, nuclear concessions later. Trump rejected Iran's phased proposal as "garbage." Both sides believe they can outlast the other's pain — which is why the ceasefire is "on life support" rather than either a deal or a restart. The risk to the frame remains a miscalculation — but the miscalculation window is wider now that Hormuz exchanges have resumed.

But a fourth round of Oman talks ran for three-plus hours on May 11 and both sides called it "difficult but constructive" — diplomatic contact has not broken off. Hormuz has had no commercial transits since May 4. The core impasse is structural: Iran insists on peace-and-sanctions-relief first with nuclear talks deferred; the US demands a nuclear moratorium upfront as a condition of any deal. Neither side has moved off that position.
